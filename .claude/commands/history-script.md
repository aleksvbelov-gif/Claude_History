## STYLE GUIDE — follow this exactly

**Structure:**
1. **[SECTION: Hook]** — Open with the most shocking or unexpected fact. Do NOT introduce the person by name yet. Drop the bombshell first. 1–2 short paragraphs.
2. **[SECTION: Introduction]** — Who this person is, why they matter, set the stakes.
3. **[SECTION: Early Life]** — Background, family, circumstances of birth and childhood.
4. **[SECTION: Rise]** — Their place in history, public role, official persona.
5. **[SECTION: The Turning Point]** — The scandal, tragedy, secret, or crisis that defines their story.
6. **[SECTION: Aftermath]** — Consequences, how the world reacted, what was hidden or suppressed.
7. **[SECTION: Final Years]** — Death or later life, what history officially records vs. what really happened.
8. **[SECTION: Legacy]** — Brief moralistic or ironic reflection. End with soft CTA: "If you found this story as fascinating as I did, make sure to subscribe — there are so many more forgotten histories waiting to be told."

**Tone:**
- Conversational yet authoritative — like a knowledgeable friend telling a gripping story
- Direct address to viewer: "As you might expect...", "Talk about a bad omen", "And here's where it gets interesting", "But here's the thing..."
- Short, punchy paragraphs — 2–4 sentences max each
- Rhetorical questions to maintain tension: "But why would she do that?"
- Foreshadow revelations: "But what happened next would shock even those closest to her"

**Language:**
- British/neutral English (not American slang)
- Specific verified details: exact dates, real names, numbers — they build credibility
- Contrast public image vs private reality throughout
- Vivid, cinematic descriptions for key moments
- No academic language — this is storytelling, not a textbook

**Length:** 3000–3500 words of narration text.

**Format:** Plain narration text with [SECTION: Name] markers. No stage directions, no camera notes, no parenthetical asides.
